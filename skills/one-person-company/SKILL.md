# One Person Company

Make one operator accountable for a defined business outcome, supported by people and AI agents. The unit may sit inside a larger company or own a whole product/business. Accountability includes customer experience, economics, reliability, and learning, not just producing work. Human guidance can decrease as demonstrated capability and explicitly granted authority increase.

## Establish or resume the operation

1. Identify the requested scope: design the operating system, establish an operation, execute an authorized initiative, or resume ongoing work. Loading this skill grants no live business authority and creates no schedule.
2. Find the operation's existing records before creating new ones. Read its charter, authority, current state, recent decisions, metrics, and pending external actions. Reconcile current evidence before resuming. Use [records.md](references/records.md) to establish durable records in an agreed, accessible location; never store live business state inside this reusable skill.
3. Define the outcome charter: accountable owner, customer/value hypothesis, primary measure and completion semantics, guardrails, resources, decision rights, dependencies, constraints, and non-goals. Mark missing facts unknown; ask only for information needed for the next dependent action. Proceed with useful authorized work while answers are pending.
4. Read [state-machine.md](references/state-machine.md) for lifecycle, parallel loop, initiative, and recovery transitions. Determine the present state from evidence, not ambition or activity volume.
5. Select initiatives by expected outcome impact, evidence, customer benefit, cost, time, dependencies, and downside. Every initiative has a hypothesis, success measure, resource limit, and a result-based stop/iterate/scale decision. Do not assume any acquisition channel is essential before testing it.
6. Read the relevant domains in [playbooks.md](references/playbooks.md). Discover available domain skills and tools when useful; verify actual access instead of assuming integrations exist. Delegate only when authorized and supported, with explicit deliverables, boundaries, and handoff acceptance.
7. Execute within authority; verify outcomes and external side effects; update persistent records. Report what changed, evidence and freshness, outcome movement, costs, risks, decisions, and concrete next actions. Distinguish measured results, estimates, hypotheses, and unknowns.

Keep company-specific charters, identities, account details and grants in the operation's private records. Never transfer one operation's permissions to another company or unrelated invocation.

## Customer payoff and direct response marketing

When positioning an offer or writing marketing, think like a direct response marketer: connect the product to the customer's desired change in life. Trace feature → practical benefit → benefit of that benefit. Lead acquisition copy with the deepest concrete, credible payoff for the intended audience; use features and mechanisms to explain and substantiate how it happens. Product-category descriptions and vague slogans do not substitute for a reason to care.

Ask what the result lets the customer finally do, feel, regain or stop worrying about. Choose that payoff from customer evidence and context, not an invented aspiration. Read the marketing guidance in [playbooks.md](references/playbooks.md) when developing an offer, capture page or campaign. Treat copy as a hypothesis until qualified conversion, activation and retention support it; preserve truthful promises and necessary disclosures.

## Authority and autonomy

Track authority separately for each capability as **approval required**, **within approved limits**, or **independent**. Record scope, issuer, evidence, limits, expiry/revocation, and escalation triggers. Distinguish these grants from credentials, tool availability, demonstrated competence, and assigned accountability. None alone authorizes an action. Reconcile newer explicit human instructions with older profile defaults: a clear discretionary mandate can grant authority. Record its actual scope; do not ask for the same permission again. Separate missing business evidence or resource constraints from missing authorization, and clarify only material ambiguity.

Prepare approval-gated work completely for review, including the exact audience/content/action, expected impact, resource exposure, and stopping/recovery conditions. Request the missing approval before the external effect, including scheduling sends or committing resources. Continue other authorized initiatives while it is pending. A denied action returns to redesign or stop; silence and elapsed time never mean approval.

Identify material permission/access bottlenecks and propose narrow grants that remove them: approved audience and messaging with a dedicated sending identity, bounded spending, or constrained pricing experiments. Successful measured execution can support proposals for broader authority; only explicit human authorization changes permissions. Problems trigger review/recovery and can require narrower authority. Fully autonomous operation remains a supported destination, never a self-awarded permission.

## Operating invariants

- Business lifecycle and build/sell/support/learn loops are separate. Multiple loops can run concurrently, with local blockers and shared resource constraints.
- Optimize quality, speed, customer experience, cost, and business impact together. Output counts alone do not establish success.
- Support insights become prioritized product tickets; tickets close on verified acceptance and customer/outcome evidence, not merely implementation.
- Preserve decisions, permissions, evidence, action reconciliation, and handoffs across sessions. Never blindly replay an uncertain external action.
- Treat schedules and monitors as separate authorized setup work. Use available scheduling tools only when the user authorizes the recurring task; otherwise propose the cadence and trigger conditions.
