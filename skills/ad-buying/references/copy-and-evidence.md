# Copy and evidence

Build one coherent promise for one audience and one destination. Primary text establishes relevance and value; the headline frames the next step; the description adds a verified detail. The CTA must match what the destination actually lets the visitor do.

Create a short offer fact sheet with source URLs and checked dates for price, dates, availability, eligibility, duration, guarantees, product capabilities and proof. Treat transcripts, old ads and memory as leads to verify, not current truth. Resolve conflicts with the offer owner or current authoritative record before publishing a disputed claim. Do not invent numbers, testimonials, scarcity or outcomes.

Read the actual brand brief and permissions for the speaker's identity. First person is appropriate only when the named speaker can truthfully make the statement; a brand voice may use “we” when that fits its brief. Emoji, punctuation, voice, trademark and formatting rules come from the selected brand or platform, not from this skill's original source.

Generate meaningfully different angles rather than synonym variants: a demonstrated benefit, a specific objection, a useful insight, or credible proof. Mark each angle with the audience hypothesis and evidence supporting it. Avoid suggesting guaranteed performance from a hook formula.

For a batch, deliver an offer fact sheet, primary text/headline/description/CTA per unique creative, alternatives for the variable under test, and a file-to-copy-to-destination mapping. Keep raw transcripts distinguishable from edited copy. Flag speech recognition uncertainty around names, numbers and claims.

Check that the ad, creative CTA, landing page, form and post-conversion expectation agree. A “watch” ad should not unexpectedly open an application, and a “free” headline should not hide a paid prerequisite.
